EEX M7G Connector

Jun 2026 – presentRustAspireProtobuf.NET

A high-priority Trayport project: a new connector to M7G, EEX’s new natural gas spot trading venue. Trayport brought together a team of experts from across the company to deliver it quickly — and I was picked to join, contributing to the connector while learning Rust.

EEX is moving its gas spot markets from a Trayport-provided platform to its own system, M7G — the latest member of the M7 trading system family, built for 24/7 trading on the gas spot markets. Go-live is targeted for the second half of 2027, pending EC approval. On the new venue every trading-software vendor connects on equal terms, so a fast, reliable native connector is what keeps Trayport’s customers trading EEX gas through Trayport. Trayport is one of the ISVs in system integration testing with EEX.

For me it is a step beyond the desktop front end of Joule into back-end, exchange-facing work, and my first production code in Rust.

I am responsible for:

  • Setting up end-to-end workflow in .NET Aspire, which runs the connector together with the services around it and desktop trading screen, so complete flows can be tested in one environment.
  • Updating the connector to follow EEX’s evolving M7G specifications as the venue takes shape ahead of go-live.
  • Working on the connector’s logging, so its behaviour can be traced and diagnosed.

Technologies used: Rust, Aspire, Protobuf, .NET
